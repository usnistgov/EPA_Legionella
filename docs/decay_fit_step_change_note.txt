Decay Fit Step-Change — Root Cause and Resolution
===================================================

Observed Symptom
----------------
For certain events (e.g., Event 105, and others including 91, 77, 75, 73, 69, 50,
48), the Predicted Ct dashed line in the PM decay figure rises sharply roughly
80–90 minutes into the 2-hour deposition window.  The effect is most dramatic for
larger-particle bins (Bin 2 through Bin 6), and the reported decay R² values for
those bins are extremely negative (e.g., R²=-1.4e+02 for Bin 4).

Root Cause
----------
The phenomenon is caused by a physically incorrect beta value (0) being used in the
forward-Euler simulation when the R²-based beta selection reaches step (c).

The indoor particle concentration during the deposition window is governed by:

    dC/dt = p·λ·C_out,t − (λ + β)·C_t

The equilibrium (steady-state) concentration that this equation approaches is:

    C_ss = p·λ·C_out / (λ + β)

When a true positive deposition rate β > 0 exists (common for larger bins where
gravitational settling and diffusion are significant), C_ss is lower than p·C_out
because the denominator is larger.  The indoor concentration decays toward this
lower equilibrium and continues to fall throughout the 2-hour window.

However, when the R²-based three-step beta selection fails to find a beta that
produces R² ≥ 0.80, step (c) previously forced β = 0.  With β = 0 the steady
state simplifies to:

    C_ss = p·C_out   (no deposition loss)

Because C_out is non-zero throughout the night/day measurement period, this
"no-deposition" equilibrium is often higher than the actual indoor concentration
after 80–90 minutes of real decay.  Once the forward-Euler simulation drives the
indoor concentration below C_ss, the infiltration term (p·λ·C_out) dominates
losses (λ·C_t), and the simulation reverses direction and rises back toward
p·C_out.  This produces the characteristic "step-change" or "elbow" shape
visible in the figure.

Example: Event 105 (2026-03-06, W40pw Open Night)
--------------------------------------------------
File: event_105-0306_w40pw_open_night_pm_decay.png
  - λ = 0.53 h⁻¹
  - Bins 0–1: R² ≥ 0.80 achieved in step (a) or (b); predicted curves behave well.
  - Bins 2–3: step (c) forced β = 0; R²(β=0) = 0.51 and 0.68 — below threshold
              but not catastrophic; mild rise visible late in window.
  - Bins 4–6: step (c) forced β = 0; R²(β=0) = −1.4×10², −1.7×10², −1.2×10²;
              the simulation rises strongly against the decaying measured data.

The true deposition rate for 1–3 µm particles can be 1–5 h⁻¹ or higher.  For
Event 105 the outdoor concentration C_out was approximately 0.01–0.05 #/cm³ and
λ = 0.53 h⁻¹, meaning the steady-state with β = 0 was ~p·0.03 ≈ 0.01–0.03 #/cm³,
whereas the measured indoor concentration at 80 min post-shower had already
decayed below ~0.01 #/cm³ for Bins 4–6.  Once below this threshold, the
simulation reversed.

Resolution (implemented 2026-03)
---------------------------------
In src/particle_calculations.py, the R²-based beta selection was extended with
a fourth outcome:

    Step (a): unclamped trimmed mean  → accept if R² ≥ 0.80
    Step (b): clamp beta to ≥ 0      → accept if R² ≥ 0.80
    Step (c): set beta = 0            → accept if R² ≥ 0.80
    Step (d): R²(β=0) < 0.80         → return beta = NaN (bin marked INVALID)

When beta = NaN the bin is treated as invalid everywhere downstream:
  - No Predicted Ct curve is drawn for that bin in the event figure.
  - The bin is rendered as a dashed, faded measured line (not solid) in the
    concentration panel to visually distinguish it from valid bins.
  - Emission rate, E_total, and Ct prediction calculations are all skipped.
  - The bin's R² is still recorded as beta_other_r_squared for diagnostics.

This eliminates the misleading rising fit lines and makes the validity of each
bin's prediction explicit in the figure.
